Build your shelf

The spice pantry guide

How to buy, store, toast and combine spices, with simple blends you can make at home for pickles, roasted vegetables and everyday cooking.

Buying

Whole, small and often

Spices lose their aroma over time as the volatile oils that carry flavour evaporate. Whole spices keep their character much longer than ground ones, so buy whole where you can and grind as needed.

  • Buy small quantities from shops with a good turnover.
  • Look for bright colour and a strong aroma when you open the jar.
  • Label each jar with the date you bought it.
Freshness

How long spices stay at their best

TypeBest withinStorage tip
Whole seeds & berriesAbout 2–3 yearsAirtight jars, away from heat and light
Ground spicesAbout 6–12 monthsSmaller jars so they’re used quickly
Dried leafy herbsAbout 1 yearCrush in your palm to test aroma
Bay leavesAbout 1–2 yearsKeep whole; snap before using

Old spices are not unsafe; they simply lose flavour. If a spice smells faint, use a little more or replace it.

Technique

Toasting and blooming

Dry-toasting whole spices in a pan for a minute or two, shaking constantly, deepens their flavour. Take them off the heat as soon as they smell fragrant.

Blooming means frying spices briefly in hot oil at the start of cooking, which carries their flavour through the whole dish.

For pickles, add whole spices straight to the jar. Crush juniper and peppercorns lightly with the flat of a knife so the brine can reach their oils.

Pickling blend

2 tbsp mustard seed, 1 tbsp coriander seed, 1 tbsp dill seed, 2 tsp peppercorns, 6 juniper berries and 3 crumbled bay leaves.

Roasting blend

1 tbsp fennel seed, 1 tbsp coriander seed, 1 tsp chilli flakes and 1 tsp black pepper, lightly crushed. Great on squash and carrots.

Northern blend

8 juniper berries, 1 tsp caraway, 1 tsp black pepper and 2 bay leaves. Simmer with cabbage, apples and onions.

Herbs

Drying your own herbs

Woody herbs such as rosemary, thyme, oregano and bay dry beautifully at home. Tie small bunches with string and hang them upside down in a warm, dry, airy spot away from direct sun.

When the leaves crumble easily, usually after one to two weeks, strip them from the stems and store in airtight jars. Tender herbs such as basil and parsley are better frozen in a little oil.

Organising

A spice shelf that works

  • Store spices in a cool, dark cupboard, not above the stove.
  • Group them by how you cook: baking, everyday savoury, pickling.
  • Use clear labels on the lid and the side for easy spotting.
  • Review once a year and refresh anything that has lost its scent.
